We first envisioned this chapter based on a simple hypothesis: open source design is about building a codebase others can push code to. An open source codebase should be readable, modular, secure, and interoperable with popular frameworks. All of these things are true and important. At the same time, we heard again and again from our interviewees that open source design is about more than just code.

Most of the projects we interviewed rarely receive regular community contributions to the core codebase. In most cases, the core codebase was maintained primarily by one to several people, with community-contributed code being fairly unusual. Nevertheless, all projects depended on community contributions of various kinds.

In this section, we’ll discuss best practices for building a readable codebase. These are practices that industry engineers are often taught on the job, and that academics often aren’t taught at all. Even relatively small projects can benefit from these practices, which promote tidy, useful code.

Next, we’ll discuss a key topic that emerged from our interviews: the different types of community contribution. We present five “contribution models”, or the five most common ways (based on our interviews) that the general public contributes their time and energy to improving open source projects. Just like open source business models (see the [Entrepreneurship section](../facet2/entrepreneurship.md)) can help you articulate how you will make money, these contribution models can help you articulate how you will attract new contributors.

From our very first interview, we had difficulty separating the topics of design and community. Eventually, we had to admit that we were trying to separate the inseparable. Open source design is about clean code, yes, but it’s also about inviting diverse folks to join your community. Part of open source design is choosing where to place the on-ramp that helps users become contributors. Placing that on-ramp in front of your codebase is just one option. Here, we’ll discuss that option plus some others.
